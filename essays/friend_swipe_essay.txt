Living in a foreign land, you practice your language skills in real life intense scenarios, like ordering a sandwich, but you never get feedback or offline ability to play around with how to do it. I thought LLMs could simulate this, and Tinder is a nice mechanism for picking up random conversations. I generated a variety of personas of people who populate a city, and I created quests to get you to speak to people successfully. People have personalities and memories. They have an opinion of you that changes. I also made stateful properties of you and characters, such as enemies, lovers, etc. The characters could be racist and not want to talk to you, just like in real life. If you really piss them off, you can get into a street fight, and police will spawn to knock you out, whereupon you appear at a local hospital. 

LLMs handle the conversations and use tools to dictate quest objectives or changes of states. Parallel LLM calls do a language critique/usage analysis that recommends improvements to how you speak (write), words you should use, words and structures you shouldn't use, etc. I use Claude Haiku for all -- cheap, fast, high quality, and good at tool use. 
Each request returns with what they say, what they now remember about you, and which way the relationship moved. I made achievements and power-up drops from completing quests. I wanted ones like peeking at someone's secret description; the ones that shipped let you take back a swipe or stamp a quest closed. 

Swiping is linear, so I took the same characters and the same conversation engine and put them into a Pokémon style 2D world. The map is generated from OpenStreetMap, so the streets have their real names, and you bump into whoever is standing nearby. The quests are real world interactions: buy bread, find a German conversation partner, meet someone who lived under Communism. I added a metro, a radio, day and night, beer that warps the camera, NPCs who brawl each other, and police. The radio streams online real local radio stations. There are movie theaters too which load random youtube videos of that language. 

Later I added Bogotá as a second city to allow Spanish, with its own 70 characters and 100 quests. The swipe app is stale now, and the walking one is where I keep working.
