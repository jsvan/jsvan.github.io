Life moves me around and sometimes I move myself too. When I sit in airplanes, I wonder about the time-dimensional "snake" that I would look like. This means if you were to reducethe time dimension into one grand holistic moment, your body would be stretching travelled paths through space. The location of your hand physically connected to its location moments ago. I think about what my snake would look like since my birth. This information is lost, but that doesn't mean I couldn't start collecting my data a few decades too late. Every hour my phone saves a location to a growing csv (with some optimizations), but I need a way to visualize it. I had been using the website www.gpsvisualizer.com, but there are two problems: A) I am giving a 3rd party my life location data, and B) its features are a bit clunky for what I need. 

The visualization can be static or animated. Points and connecting lines can be toggled independently, colors changed, opacities, etc. 

By playing with location dot sizes and setting the opacity real low, we receive a "heatmap" of your favourite places. But this is imperfect because only shared points are expressed. We are missing favourite paths. When I take a route every day to work, my location will be sampled at the source and destination, and perhaps once or twice randomly in between. Yet these points are sparse along this path, so they essentially get erased. How can we stitch paths together from among independent trips? And how can we do this such that, if I had two or three roads I could take to work, the algorithm could probabilistically separate them out and show each one individually, rather than some zig zag? So that's what I'm currently working on. I found this research paper that handles this algorithm. 
https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/KDD12-PopularRoutes.pdf



This site lets you upload a similar CSV of form:

- one location per line,
- csv
- ordered as date, time, latitude, longitude

- `date, time, latitude, longitude`
- `4/2/24, 08:00:03, 40.4167754, -3.7037902`
- `15.06.2025, 18:00:01, -33.8688197, 151.2092955`

  - Latitude has to be between −90 and 90, longitude between −180 and 180 
  - Dates can be any of the following (if any slash date has a first number above 12, all slash dates in the file are read day-first instead): 
    - M/D/YY or M/D/YYYY
    - D.M.YYYY
    - YYYY-MM-DD
    - Two-digit years become 20xx.
  - Times can be H:MM or H:MM:SS, 24-hour or with AM/PM (with no time, the point is placed at midnight)
  - A line whose date can't be read is dropped
  - Order doesn't matter. Points are sorted by time before drawing, so the animation always runs in time order.
